# Pick WiFi hologram fans, skip HDMI

For a food truck cocktail display in Australia, the best small pick is the **Holofex HoloXG2C 42 cm with safety cover (about $184–197)**. It is the only budget fan found that has an Australian warehouse, a 12-month warranty and a cover in the box. The best large pick is a **generic 65 cm WiFi fan, either the Amazon AU "Z3H 65cm" (about AUD 312) or the eBay AU 65 cm WiFi unit (from about AUD 340), plus a separate 65 cm cover (about AUD 171)**. That puts the large setup at roughly **AUD 480–510, right at the budget ceiling**. No fan in either size has live HDMI input for under AUD 500. **Real HDMI-input fans start at about 65 cm and cost about USD 1,200 (roughly AUD 1,700+) before shipping and GST.** They are a separate product line with an external HDMI controller box, and you cannot add HDMI to a cheap fan later. Within budget, the Pi does not stream video to the fan. Instead it **chooses among clips already loaded on the fan's card**, using one of three methods: replaying the IR remote's signals, sending WiFi commands (open-source work exists for one fan family) or switching power with a relay. Small fans usually run on **12 V DC at about 15–25 W**. Large 65–70 cm fans use **24 V supplies of up to about 96 W**. Both can run from a food truck's 12 V system with the right converter. **Every price in this report is a search-engine snapshot taken on 19 October 2026. The live listing pages could not be opened to check them, and some show a bare "$" with no currency.** Check every price, currency, connection type and cover option on the live page before you buy.

## Two fans fit the brief, one comfortably and one barely

The small band is easy. The better buy is the **Holofex HoloXG2C**: 42 cm, 384 LEDs, 2,400 nits, WiFi and Bluetooth, with a safety cover included. It showed at **$184 (marked down from $341)**, and other cached pages showed $190–197 ([Holofex HoloXG2C](https://holofex.com/en-us/products/holoxg2c-42cm-cover)). ## True HDMI starts at 65 cm and about AUD 1,700

If you truly need live HDMI input, you are buying a different class of product. Every genuine HDMI fan found is **65 cm or larger** and sold as its own "HDMI version" SKU. The PC connects by HDMI to a **separate control box, which then links to the fan by fibre** ([Missyou MS-HDMI](https://missyou.net/collections/missyou-ms-hdmi)). The cheapest are the HDFocus 65 cm on Alibaba at **USD 1,215 for 1–9 units** ([Alibaba HDFocus](https://www.alibaba.com/product-detail/HDFocus-Hdmi-Hologram-Fan-65-Cm_1601255943514.html)), Missyou MS-HDMI from USD 1,215 and Shenzhen SMX at USD 1,200 ([SMX listing](https://www.hologram3ddisplay.com/sale-52140880-hdmi-hologram-fan-65-cm-3d-fan-hologram-display-hdmi-synchronization-with-computer-interactive-3d-ho.html)). At about 1.43 AUD per USD, that is **about AUD 1,700 before freight and GST**. Branded retail units cost more. The Holofex HOLOHD65 is USD 1,749, about AUD 2,500, and includes a dedicated HDMI controller ([Holofex HOLOHD65](https://holofex.com/en-us/products/holohd65)). The Holofan Co HDMI 25.6" is USD 1,600–1,800, and its box contents list no cover ([Holofan Co](https://holofanco.com/products/holofan%C2%AE-hdmi-3d-hologram-fan-25-inch)). No HDMI box was found for sale on its own, so a cheap fan cannot be upgraded later. Be sceptical of AliExpress "65 cm HDMI" listings priced in the low hundreds of USD. The established HDMI sellers cluster at USD 1,200, which suggests the cheap listings are WiFi fans with misleading titles.

"USB" on budget fans does not mean a live display either. No consumer fan was found with a verified mode that mirrors a PC screen live over a USB cable. On cheap fans, "PC software" and "sync" mean uploading files or keeping several fans in time with each other. The only live-stream software found runs on **Windows only** ([SuperbHolo downloads](https://www.superbholo.com/download-resources/)). Most budget fans also play a **proprietary .bin format**, not raw MP4. The vendor app converts MP4 on the phone before uploading, so "supports MP4" in a listing usually describes the app, not the card ([Virtual On FAQ](https://virtualongroup.com/faq-items/type-video-file-plays/); [SuperbHolo manual](https://www.superbholo.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/SH-30-User-manual.pdf)).

## A Raspberry Pi selects preloaded clips rather than streaming them

Within budget, the reliable design is simple. **Load all cocktail clips onto the fan's TF card once, name them in order (`000001.bin`, `000002.bin` and so on), set the fan to single-loop, and have the Pi choose which clip plays.** The fan sorts filenames byte by byte, so the leading zeros fix each clip's play ID ([FanPlugin.Wrapper](https://github.com/buzzibaer/FanPlugin.Wrapper)). You can still make new content on the Pi. The open-source **holo-fan-linux** tool converts any video FFmpeg can read into the "EE37" .bin format and copies it to the card. It has been confirmed on just one model, the **Missyou MS3 (F1) 32 cm** ([holo-fan-linux](https://github.com/zackmcmurrin-dev/holo-fan-linux)). The Michdo93 project includes an encoder for the "3D_42CM" fan family, but its output is not byte-identical to the vendor app's ([Michdo93](https://github.com/Michdo93/hologram-fan-projector)). No open project can upload files to a fan over WiFi yet. Changing the playlist still means moving the card or using the vendor app.

There are three ways for the Pi to control playback, listed from most to least universal. **IR remote replay** works on any fan that comes with a remote. You record each button with LIRC's `irrecord` and replay it with `irsend` from an IR LED on a GPIO pin ([partofthething](https://partofthething.com/thoughts/adding-a-ir-send-and-receive-capabilities-to-my-home-assistant-based-smart-home/)), or use IRremote's `sendRaw` on an Arduino ([Arduino Forum](https://forum.arduino.cc/t/duplicating-fan-remote-with-ir-transmitter/979177)). It needs no network, but it can only step next or previous. **WiFi TCP control** can jump straight to a clip by its ID. The Michdo93 script uses only Python's standard library. It talks to fans whose hotspot is named like `3D_42CM_xxxx`, at `192.168.4.1:20320`, and supports power, next/previous, brightness, loop mode and per-clip duration. It needs a connection that stays open with keepalives, and it may lock out the phone app ([Michdo93](https://github.com/Michdo93/hologram-fan-projector)). In practice the Pi needs a **second USB WiFi adapter** with a static address of 192.168.4.2 to join the fan's hotspot ([FanPlugin.Wrapper](https://github.com/buzzibaer/FanPlugin.Wrapper)). A third protocol on port 50200, used by "3D Magic"-app fans, appears in a community post but is unconfirmed ([Flic community](https://community.flic.io/topic/18371/remote-for-hologram-fan)). **A relay or smart plug** handles the daily on and off. Fans start looping their content automatically when powered ([HDFocus guide](https://www.hd-focus.com/info/how-to-work-a-3d-hologram-fan-a-beginner-s-st-103458601.html)). Combined with IR stepping, a power cycle also resets the fan to a known clip. Keep relay switching to once a day rather than once per clip, because repeated hard stops strain a spinning rotor. That last point is engineering judgement, not a sourced finding. One gap matters for this design: **no source says whether a fan resumes its last clip or restarts at clip 1 after a power cut**, so test this on your unit before writing the control script.

For the small band, this points to a tinkerer's alternative to the Holofex: a generic 42 cm fan whose hotspot appears as `3D_42CM_xxxx`, which the WiFi script supports, or a Missyou 32 cm, which the converter supports. Missyou also sells a 32 cm fan with a clear protective cover ([Missyou 32 cm with cover](https://missyou.net/products/32cm-desktop-style-3dhologramfan)). Whether the Holofex XG2C or the Z3H uses either protocol family is unknown. Plan on IR control for them unless the seller confirms otherwise.

## 12 V suits small fans, and 24 V needs a converter

Most fans come with a 100–240 VAC adapter that feeds DC to the fan, so they run on Australian mains, or on a truck's DC supply if you bypass the adapter. A typical 43 cm generic takes **12 V DC at 15–25 W** ([eBay.de listing](https://www.ebay.de/itm/146564261443)). Missyou's 42 cm is rated 15–20 W ([Missyou manual](https://manuals.plus/asin/B08MWQQHCD)). A small fan can therefore run straight off the truck's 12 V system once you match the plug and polarity. Large fans run on 24 V. Missyou's replacement supply for 65–100 cm fans is rated **24 V 4 A, so up to about 96 W** ([Missyou power cord](https://missyou.net/products/3d-fan-dedicated-power-cord)). On a 12 V truck that means a **12-to-24 V DC-DC boost converter rated about 150 W for headroom** (100 W at minimum), or running the stock adapter from an inverter. A Pi 4 adds about 5–15 W through a 12-to-5 V converter. China-shipped units often arrive with US or EU plugs ([Amazon US NDNCZDHC](https://www.amazon.com/Hologram-Holographic-Advertising-Machine-Projection/dp/B0CK27VBMV)). **No listing found states RCM approval.** For a commercial vehicle, a separately bought RCM-marked supply of matching voltage and current is the safer choice. A cover is more than a nice extra on a food truck. Exposed spinning blades at customer height, plus grease, dust and wind, are exactly what the clear housing protects against. That is why it belongs in the budget from day one.

### Pre-purchase checklist

| Check | What to confirm with the seller or listing |
|---|---|
| Live price and currency | Is the "$" AUD or USD? What is the landed total, including shipping and GST? |
| Exact model and cover fit | For Holofex, order the XG2C, not the GX2. For a 65 cm fan, confirm the cover size is in stock and fits that model. |
| Connection reality | Is the "HDMI" a live display input or file upload? Is "USB" a flash-drive slot, a TF card or a PC cable? |
| File format | Does the fan play raw MP4 from the card, or only vendor .bin files? |
| Remote included | Is there an IR remote, which the Pi can replay? |
| WiFi family | What is the hotspot SSID (`3D_42CM_…`?) and IP (192.168.4.1 or 10.10.10.1)? Which app does it use? |
| Power | What are the DC voltage and current at the fan (12 V or 24 V), and the connector type? |
| Power-loss behaviour | Does it resume the last clip or restart at clip 1? Test on arrival. |
| Compliance and plug | Is it RCM-marked with an AU plug? If not, budget for an RCM supply. |
| Warranty and returns | Is support local (Holofex, Amazon AU returns) or marketplace buyer protection only? |

## Conclusion

The budget changes what the project is. At AUD 500 you are buying a **self-contained video player that the Pi directs**, not a display that the Pi drives. Once you accept that, a fan with an IR remote and a TF card serves a cocktail menu almost as well as HDMI would, at about a fifth of the cost. The pieces most likely to cause trouble are not the fans but the extras: cover availability for 65 cm units, DC voltage on the truck, and how each unit behaves after a power cut. Those should guide the purchase more than LED counts or "4K" claims. A sensible next step is to buy the Holofex XG2C first and use it to prove the IR or WiFi control script and the 12 V wiring before spending close to the limit on a 65 cm unit and cover. HDMI is worth revisiting only if live content, such as a camera feed or menus that change by the minute, turns out to be essential. At that point the realistic price is about AUD 1,700–2,500.
