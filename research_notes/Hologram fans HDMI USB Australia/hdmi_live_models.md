WebFetch and curl both failed on aiholofan.com, amazon.com, made-in-china.com, acevsn.com and fursol.com. So everything below comes from search-engine extracts of the listed pages, not from reading the full pages. Currency conversion uses roughly **1 USD = 1.43 AUD**, the latest rate found: 1.4384 on 2026-07-29 and 1.4292 on 2026-07-22, per [Wise](https://wise.com/gb/currency-converter/usd-to-aud-rate?amount=1) and [Halo Financial](https://halofinancial.com/currency-markets/usd-to-aud-exchange-rate). ## Which fans have HDMI input built in, and which need an add-on HDMI module or box? What does the add-on cost?

### Takeaway
Real HDMI-input fans only exist from about **65 cm (25.6") upward**. They are sold as a separate "HDMI version" SKU that comes with a dedicated HDMI controller or sync box, often connected to the fan by fibre. They cost roughly **USD 1,200–1,800 (about AUD 1,700–2,600) per 65 cm unit**, well over the 500 AUD budget. I found **no HDMI-input fan in the 30–45 cm band**, and no add-on HDMI box sold separately with a price for a regular consumer fan.

### Inferences
- Within the 500 AUD budget there is **no genuine HDMI-input hologram fan** at either size. The cheapest real HDMI option found is about USD 1,200 (about AUD 1,700+ before shipping and GST) for 65 cm.
- The "HDMI fan" is in practice a **different product line**: a fan with a high-bandwidth receiver, plus an external HDMI controller box, often fibre-linked. It is not an add-on you can retrofit to a cheap WiFi fan. I found no evidence that a consumer fan can be upgraded with an "HDMI box".
- Several brands (Missyou MS-HDMI, HDFocus, SMX, AIHOLO) share the same USD 1,200–1,215 price point and similar specs. They are likely the same OEM hardware sold under different names, but I have not verified this.

### Gaps
- No standalone price for an "HDMI sync box" or controller sold separately was found anywhere.
- No small (30–45 cm) HDMI model was found. Whether one is available as a custom factory order is unknown.
- Whether HDMI-model prices include the box, fibre cable and cover was not confirmed. Australian landed cost (shipping, GST, plug) is unknown.

## Which fans support live "PC sync" or "real-time display" over USB or WiFi, what software does it need, and does it work on Linux or Raspberry Pi?

### Takeaway
I found **no consumer fan with a verified live USB-cable PC mirroring mode**. On cheap 42–65 cm fans, "PC software" and "synchronization" mean **uploading files** or **syncing several fans together**, not showing a live screen. Where live PC streaming software exists, it is **Windows-only** (SuperbHolo's "Live Stream PC Software (For Windows)", tied to model SH-A80; HDFocus PC control software). No Linux or macOS client was found.

### Cited Findings
- SuperbHolo's download page lists "Live Stream PC Software (For Windows) – Apply to SH-A80". The SH-A80 is described as a 2022 industrial fan with 1280P resolution, a "Live Stream function" and cloud service. SuperbHolo's live-stream workflow involves filming a subject against green or blue cloth with a phone or PC camera, so it is a camera feed and not necessarily desktop mirroring. — [superbholo downloads](https://www.superbholo.com/download-resources/); [superbholo SH-A80](https://www.superbholo.com/3d-hologram-fan-sh-a80/); [superbholo SH-HDMI](https://www.superbholo.com/3d-hologram-fan-sh-hdmi/)
- HOLOFAN Co. offers "Windows Software" alongside its iOS and Android apps for its standard fans. — [holofanco HOLOFAN](https://holofanco.com/collections/holofan/products/holofan-1)
- The Missyou MS4 manual says Missyou "provides software for PC mode editing", which is content editing, not a live mode. — [manuals.plus Missyou MS4](https://manuals.plus/missyou/missyou-ms4-3d-hologram-fan-wifi-projector)

Some list USB as connectivity, but nothing says USB carries a live stream. — [Amazon B0DH4PDVTJ](https://www.amazon.com/hologram-Holographic-Resolution-Christmas-Advertising/dp/B0DH4PDVTJ)

- An AliExpress article on multi-unit 42 cm setups says content is uploaded once, auto-distributed to each projector's internal memory, and the fans "cannot stream live feeds remotely". This is a low-quality blog-style source. — [AliExpress wiki article](https://www.aliexpress.com/s/wiki-ssr/article/hologram-advertising-display)
- Coolify's own holo fans load content over WiFi from an app. — [Coolify guide](https://coolifycool.com/3-steps-to-customize-the-image-on-your-holo-fan/)

### Inferences
- If a cheap listing says "PC" or "sync", assume file upload or multi-fan sync unless it explicitly says live screen mirroring. Ask the seller before buying.
- Because the live-stream software is Windows-only, a Raspberry Pi cannot run the vendor's live software natively. A Pi can only drive a live display through an HDMI-input fan's controller box. That makes the Pi just an HDMI source, which should work for any 1080p-capable box, but no vendor documents it specifically.

### Gaps
- No manual or independent test confirms a USB-cable live mode on any fan.
- The exact OS requirements, version and whether SuperbHolo's SH-A80 software does desktop capture are unknown.
- I found no Linux, Wine or Pi reports for any vendor's PC software.

## How does a Raspberry Pi realistically drive each option?

### Takeaway
There are three realistic paths:
1. **HDMI-version fan (65 cm+, about AUD 1,700+):** Pi HDMI out goes into the vendor's HDMI controller box, which links to the fan by fibre or cable. This gives true live output.
2. **Cheap WiFi/TF-card fan (42–65 cm, under 500 AUD):** pre-convert content to the fan's format, then copy it to the SD/TF card or upload it through the vendor app. Not live.
3. **Vendor Windows live software:** not usable from a Pi, so it needs a Windows PC.

### Cited Findings
- The HDMI-box topology (PC → HDMI → control box → fibre → fan, with HDFocus PC control software) is described for HDFocus-type HDMI fans. — [missyou MS-HDMI](https://missyou.net/collections/missyou-ms-hdmi)
- SuperbHolo HDMI models act as plain HDMI displays with no app or audio, and the PC software is used only to change the live-streaming area. — [superbholo SH-HDMI](https://www.superbholo.com/3d-hologram-fan-sh-hdmi/)
- Consumer fans take content by TF card or app upload. — [Amazon B0DH4PDVTJ](https://www.amazon.com/hologram-Holographic-Resolution-Christmas-Advertising/dp/B0DH4PDVTJ); [Newegg 42cm](https://www.newegg.com/p/0FC-065A-003S0)

### Inferences
- With path 1, the HDMI box most likely crops or scales a region of the Pi's desktop into the fan's circular area. SuperbHolo mentions choosing the "live streaming area" in PC software, which suggests the box may need Windows setup once even if a Pi drives it afterwards. Not verified.
- Path 2 is the only option under budget. The Pi could automate it by writing MP4s to the SD card or pushing them over the fan's WiFi hotspot. But the fan apps are proprietary, so automating WiFi upload would need reverse engineering, and no documentation was found.

### Gaps
- No vendor documents Raspberry Pi compatibility, or supported HDMI input resolution and refresh (for example 1080p60 versus 4K), for the boxes.

## Known reliability and latency issues reported by users

### Takeaway
I found **no independent user reports** (Reddit, forums, reviews) on HDMI live-mode lag, stutter or reliability. The only latency figures are seller claims: AIHOLO "<100 ms" on its spec sheet versus "zero latency" in its marketing copy.

### Cited Findings
- AIHOLO lists HDMI latency as <100 ms and 50 FPS, while its marketing says "zero latency". — [aiholofan product](https://aiholofan.com/product/hdmi-65cm-holographic-fan/); [aiholofan 1x3 wall blog](https://aiholofan.com/1x3-hdmi-holographic-fan-wall-real-time-life-size-human-display-with-zero-latency/)
- The Coolify roundup says advanced setups (multi-sync, live HDMI) "can be technically involved". — [Coolify 2025 roundup](https://coolifycool.com/hologram-3d-fan-displays-2025-roundup-and-buying-guide/)
- A search aimed at Reddit user experiences returned only retail listings. — (search conducted 2026-10-19; no qualifying source)

### Inferences
- Treat the "4K" and "3840×1280" claims sceptically. LED count sets the real resolution: Holofex says resolution depends on the number of LEDs on the blades ([holofex blog](https://holofex.com/en-us/blogs/holographic-tech/what-resolution-do-holographic-fans-have-understanding-led-resolution)), and 960–1,280 LEDs across a 65 cm blade cannot deliver true 4K.

### Gaps
- No independent latency measurements, failure-rate data or long-term reliability reports were found for HDMI or live-mode fans.
- Full Amazon Q&A and reviews could not be read because fetches were blocked.
