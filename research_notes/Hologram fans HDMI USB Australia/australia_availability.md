# Australian Availability of LED Hologram Fans (small 30–45 cm, large 56–70 cm, under AUD 500)

> **Method caveat (important for the report writer):** In this research session, direct page fetches were blocked by the sandbox network (amazon.com.au, ebay.com.au, kogan.com, holofex.com, missyou.net all returned DNS/proxy refusals). All figures below come from **search-engine snippets of the listed pages**, which are often cached and may be stale. Currency is frequently shown as a bare "$". Treat every price as an **unverified listing snapshot (searched 19 Oct 2026)** that needs checking on the live page before purchase.

## What do Amazon.com.au, eBay Australia, Kogan, Catch, AliExpress, Temu and AU specialist sellers offer, at what prices?

### Takeaway
Small (~42 cm / 16.5") generic fans are plentiful on Amazon AU and eBay AU at roughly AUD 50–100, usually shipped from China. Large (56–70 cm) fans under AUD 500 do exist (eBay AU 65 cm WiFi from ~AUD 340; Amazon AU Z3H 65 cm ~AUD 312), but they are WiFi/app models. **HDMI fans at 65 cm cost about USD 1,000+ and are well over the AUD 500 budget.** Kogan and Catch had no hologram fan listings in search results.

## Typical power draw / input voltage (12V vs 240V) and AU plug/RCM compliance

### Takeaway
Small 42 cm fans typically run from a 100–240 VAC external adapter feeding a low-voltage DC fan (often 12 V), drawing about 10–25 W. Large 65–70 cm fans use around 24 V DC adapters (Missyou's cord is rated 24 V 4 A, i.e. up to ~96 W). Because the fan itself runs on DC, 12 V or 24 V vehicle-power operation is plausible with a suitable DC supply. **No listing found stated RCM marking.**

### Inferences
- Most units accept 100–240 V, so they work on Australian 230 V mains via an AU plug or adapter. China/US-shipped units often come with US/EU plugs, so a proper AU adapter or locally sourced AU-plug adapter is needed.
- For food-truck 12 V use: small fans with a 12 V DC input could run from 12 V directly (check polarity and connector). 24 V large fans need a 12→24 V DC-DC boost converter (≥100 W) or an inverter. Inverter draw is modest (~20–100 W).
- RCM compliance (general background, not sourced in this session): Australian suppliers of in-scope electrical equipment are expected to carry RCM marking, and the external mains adapter is the part most relevant. Grey-import marketplace units are unlikely to be RCM-registered. Buyers should ask the seller, or use a separately purchased RCM-marked power supply of matching voltage and amperage.

- Exact connectivity per AU listing (USB-stick vs TF card vs USB-cable PC mode) was not verifiable beyond Holofex's statement of USB/HDMI upload. Generic WiFi fans typically use TF card plus app, but this was not confirmed per listing.
